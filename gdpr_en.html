1. Principles of Data Processing

The StakeForge system follows the principle of minimal data collection. We only store data that is strictly necessary for the operation of the platform and user identification.

1. Data Controller Information and Principles

The StakeForge system follows the principle of data minimization. We only collect and process data that is strictly essential for the operation of the platform and the identification of users.

Contact and Support: support@stake-forge.com

Data Processor: For the secure storage of user data and database infrastructure, the platform utilizes the encrypted cloud services of Supabase Inc. (1355 Market St, Suite 488, San Francisco, CA 94103) as a third-party data processor.

  • Identifiers: Username and email address.
  • Security: Encrypted password (stored as a hash – raw passwords are never accessible).
  • Balances: Current DEMO and REAL account balances.
  • Financial data: A unique BTC wallet address assigned to the account (if eligibility applies).
  • Technical data: geographic location (used exclusively for access control verification and geo-blocking purposes; IP addresses are not stored).

    • 3. Restrictions (Geo-blocking)

    The service provider applies geographical restrictions in accordance with applicable laws. Users connecting from Hungarian IP addresses are not allowed to make deposits for REAL balances or generate BTC wallet addresses. These users may only use DEMO functionality.

    4. Data Security and Third Parties

    Your data is stored on the encrypted Supabase cloud platform. Personal data (email address, username) is not shared with or sold to third parties. Data is used exclusively for authentication, account management, and platform operation.

    5. Your Rights

    You may request export, modification, or permanent deletion of your data at any time. In case of deletion, all personal data (email, wallet address) will be removed from our system. Transaction logs may still be retained in anonymized form for statistical purposes.
